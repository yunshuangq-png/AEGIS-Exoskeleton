# PROJECT AEGIS
### Human Augmentation through Wearable Robotics

**Project Type:** Independent Engineering Research  
**Research Area:** Wearable Robotics, Biomechatronics, Human-Machine Interaction  
**Status:** V1 — Mechanical Feasibility

---

## 1. Research Motivation

Human augmentation explores how technological systems can extend human physical capabilities beyond their unaided limits.

Wearable robotic systems provide a potential means of augmenting human movement, strength, and physical interaction with the environment.

However, designing an effective upper-limb exoskeleton requires more than generating mechanical force. It also requires careful consideration of biomechanical compatibility, mechanical transmission, user intention, and the relationship between humans and machines.

Project AEGIS is an independent engineering research project investigating these challenges through the design and development of tendon-driven wearable robotic systems.

## 2. Central Research Question

How can wearable robotic systems augment human upper-limb physical capabilities while maintaining natural movement and biomechanical compatibility?

## 3. Research Questions

**RQ1 — Mechanical Augmentation**

How can mechanical transmission systems be designed to generate useful joint torque while minimizing unnecessary mechanical complexity and distal mass?

**RQ2 — Intention Recognition**

How can wearable sensors identify human movement intention and support responsive robotic assistance or augmentation?

**RQ3 — Human-Machine Interface**

How can wearable robotic interfaces maintain biomechanical compatibility while minimizing discomfort, pressure, and unwanted relative movement?

**RQ4 — Embodied Agency**

How does interaction with an augmentative robotic system influence a person's perception of bodily capability, autonomy, and bodily boundaries?

## 4. Project Development

### V1 — Tendon-Driven Elbow Testbed

Investigate the relationship between tendon routing geometry, joint angle, tendon force, and generated elbow torque using a non-wearable benchtop testbed.

### V2 — Active Wearable Augmentation

Explore actuator integration, wearable mechanical structures, and basic movement-intention recognition.

### V3 — Human-Machine Integration

Investigate biomechanical compatibility, compliant interfaces, and the interaction between the user and the robotic system.

### V4 — Integrated Research System

Develop and evaluate an integrated upper-limb augmentation prototype.

## 5. Current Focus: V1

**Initial Research Question:**

How does tendon routing geometry affect the torque generated at a single-degree-of-freedom elbow joint across different joint angles?

**Initial Hypotheses:**

- H1: Changes in tendon moment arm affect the torque generated at the elbow joint.
- H2: The relationship between tendon force and joint torque changes with joint angle and tendon routing geometry.

**Initial Experimental Approach:**

- Construct a non-wearable mechanical elbow testbed.
- Measure tendon force and joint angle.
- Compare different tendon routing configurations.
- Compare theoretical predictions with experimental measurements.
